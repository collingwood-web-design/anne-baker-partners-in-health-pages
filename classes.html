Classes in Collingwood

Weekly classes for people who want a place to go.

If you like a set time and other people in the room, these are the classes I am teaching now. They are in Collingwood, not online. Fundamental Fitness and Circuit Pilates are at Active Life Conditioning, where I also work.

Fundamental Fitness

A high-beginner to intermediate class: short intervals, balance, strength, and mobility, aimed at staying strong and supporting longevity.

Wednesdays and Fridays, 10:30 to 11:30 a.m. at Active Life Conditioning, Collingwood.

See Fundamental Fitness and register

Circuit Pilates

A slow to moderate Pilates mat class. Exercises are repeated so your nervous system can learn them, and so you can feel how Pilates shows up in ordinary movement. Blocks, rings, and bands are part of the class.

Mondays, 10:30 to 11:30 a.m. at Active Life Conditioning, Collingwood.

Active Life Conditioning class listings

Mobility class

A slower class for body awareness, breathing, balance, mobility, stability, and a calmer nervous system. It is also a place to see people. You need to be able to get down to the floor and back up. A chair, yoga blocks, and your own mat are used. The mat is not supplied.

Wednesdays, 2:30 to 3:30 p.m. at the Collingwood Leisure Time Club.

Members $15. Non-members $17.

Questions

Who are the classes for?

People who want strength, balance, and mobility rather than an all-out workout. The Leisure Time Club class is a slower class for anyone who wants to stay active.

Do I book these through Anne?

Fundamental Fitness and Circuit Pilates run at Active Life Conditioning. The slower mobility class runs at the Collingwood Leisure Time Club. Use their links to see the current schedule and to register. I teach the classes.

What if I want something more personal?

Book personal training, a partner session, or a small group with me directly. Those can be at home or online.

I help people stay strong and active through every stage of life, in person around South Georgian Bay and online anywhere.

Land acknowledgement

For more than 15,000 years the First Nations walked upon, and cared for, the lands we now call home: Anishinaabek, Haudenosaunee, Ojibwe, Petun, Wendat and many others who cared for their families and communities, the way we now seek to care for ours. Partners in Health acknowledge the Lake Simcoe-Nottawasaga Treaty of 1818 and respects all the Nation-to-Nation agreements that have formed relationships with the original inhabitants of Turtle Island. We seek to do better, to continue to recognize, learn, and grow, in friendship and community, Nation-to-Nation.
